CSE 435: Team Lane Management Systems 1

Project Background:

A key component of being a driver is being able to acknowledge differences in lanes and their purposes on the highway or public roads. However, not every environment that a driver will usually be in makes it easy to identify lane markings on the road. The implementation of a Lane Management System addresses safer driving by detecting lanes and properly adhering to road rules through lane detection. As the automotive industry continues to evolve, new features are being created to ensure safety and security for future drivers which allows for the expansion of Lane Management systems.

Project Description:

The Lane Management System (LMS) is a driver assistance system that determines which lane a vehicle is in and where the vehicle sits within the lane, as well as the drivers future position in order to act to keep the driver in their lane. This project has three main features: a Lane Departure Warning System that alerts the driver if their vehicle begins to drift out of the lane, a Lane Keeping System that steers to correct a lane departure that was unintended, and a Lane Centering System that helps position the vehicle properly within the lane.

The system has cameras for lane marker detetcing and vehicle sensors for speed, steering angles, and road curvature, which helps to predict the vehicles path and flag issues. A supervisory controller decides when each feature is active because they all only operate when certain thresholds are met. A large design challenge is drivers authority: when should the system take control from the driver, how and when can the driver override it, and how does the system behave when markings are unclear.
